# Facial branches teaching example

Download `original-lecture.pptx` and `objectives.xlsx`, then choose **Build from slides + objectives** in Lesson Builder. Import both files and select the illustration on **slide 48**. Review the image, educator answer keys, source and image licence before creating the lesson.

The workbook contains one objective for each of High school, Undergrad, Step 1, Post-Step 1, Step 2 and Resident. These are new adaptations of task complexity, not validated board-level competencies or official examination alignment. The workbook's **Guide and sources** tab explains its use and attribution.

## Original lecture and learner illustration

Slide 48 of **C. Cranial Nerve Lecture.pptx** contains the facial-nerve illustration, an overlaid title and branch-name list, and the original image credit. Its related speaker notes supply the parotid relationship and further attribution. The workbook references that original slide number in every row.

The importer extracts the embedded 715 × 599 JPEG. The JPEG itself has no text labels; it is not a screenshot of the labelled slide. PowerPoint overlays, notes and slide formatting do not become learner pixels. Keep the original lecture and workbook answer keys as educator references. This is a normal anatomy example with no patient history, finding, lesion or diagnosis supplied.

## Teaching limits

No independent clinical review is claimed for this adaptation. The source supports the named terminal branches, their motor role and branching within the parotid gland. It does not supply patient-specific diagnosis, individual variant anatomy, surgical-risk estimates or treatment guidance.

Objective evidence is formative feedback on a learner attempt. Assisted same-case performance does not establish independent retention, transfer, mastery or prevention of deskilling.
