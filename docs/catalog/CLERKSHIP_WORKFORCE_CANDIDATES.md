# CaseAttend: 60 public-domain case-learning candidates for Step 2, clerkships, rotations, and the clinical workforce

Verified 2026-08-09. This is a research and curriculum-design backlog, not medical advice. Each vignette seed below is newly authored; source pages are for fact-checking, not for copying prose. If CaseAttend's maintainers own the new lesson expression, they can choose to dedicate the resulting lesson JSON/text to CC0. The cited federal source material is U.S. public-domain content under the evidence linked in each entry; CC0 and U.S. public domain are legally different mechanisms.

Verification method: each exact source and policy URL was resolved through the official page or a current official search-index record, and the selected page text showed no item-level copyright/third-party exception notice. NINDS currently rejects some automated direct fetches, but its current official search records resolve the listed page URLs and its blanket policy states that all NINDS website information is public domain. A future exception notice is a merge blocker.

## Reuse rules used for this audit

- Reuse **text/facts only**. Do not import source-page photos, illustrations, videos, logos, screenshots, brand marks, linked journal articles, embedded third-party tools, or externally hosted assets.
- Write the case, answer choices, feedback, and objectives from scratch. Preserve clinical meaning, add source links, and do not imply agency endorsement.
- CDC-derived lessons must credit CDC, state that the source is available free at the linked page, and include a no-endorsement notice. Link to the live source because CDC guidance changes.
- NIMH expressly prohibits reuse of its website/publication images. NIDDK, NHLBI, NICHD, and CDC also flag third-party or restricted graphics. NINDS text is public domain, but linked scales/tools may carry separate rights.
- AHRQ lessons below use documents with an item-level **Public Domain Notice**. Do not reproduce materials within them that are separately marked copyrighted.
- Every candidate needs discipline review, accessibility QA, bias review, and date-sensitive guideline review before merge. Do not ship drug doses, treatment thresholds, or legal rules without an appropriately credentialed reviewer.

## Internal medicine and Step 2

### CA-IM-001 : The normal first troponin

- **Level/role:** Step 2; internal-medicine and emergency-medicine clerk; RN triage reinforcement.
- **Specialty:** Internal medicine, cardiology, emergency medicine, triage.
- **Artifact type:** Text-first, time-stepped diagnostic vignette with a labeled ECG summary and serial lab table.
- **Original vignette seed:** A 59-year-old delivery driver has 40 minutes of pressure-like chest discomfort and sweating. The first ECG has no diagnostic ST elevation and the first troponin is within the reference range; discomfort continues.
- **Learning objectives:** (1) Treat a time-zero ECG and troponin as snapshots rather than definitive exclusion; (2) prioritize repeat assessment and serial testing; (3) compare immediately dangerous cardiac and noncardiac alternatives without delaying escalation.
- **Exact source URL:** [NHLBI : Heart Attack Diagnosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart-attack/diagnosis).
- **License evidence:** **NHLBI-PD**, [exact policy](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/about/contact/trademark-branding-and-logo) : “most ... information ... is in the public domain.”
- **Attribution:** Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute; National Institutes of Health; U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
- **Neutral accessible description:** A person with persistent chest pressure is reassessed as ECG and biomarker results change; all values and timing are available as screen-reader-friendly text, not color cues.
- **Review caveats:** Do not create or copy an ECG image from NHLBI; use an original textual tracing interpretation or a separately licensed ECG. Cardiology/EM review is required for timing, assay language, and disposition.

### CA-IM-002 : Breathless after missing medicines

- **Level/role:** Step 2; internal-medicine clerk; RN/respiratory-therapy handoff.
- **Specialty:** Internal medicine, cardiology, inpatient care.
- **Artifact type:** Branching admission case with history, examination, medication list, and plain-data results.
- **Original vignette seed:** A 72-year-old with known reduced cardiac function stopped several medicines during a move and now has progressive exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, ankle swelling, and new oxygen need.
- **Learning objectives:** (1) Build a syndrome-level differential for congestion; (2) identify likely precipitants such as medication interruption, ischemia, arrhythmia, infection, and renal dysfunction; (3) select initial tests and monitoring while recognizing respiratory instability.
- **Exact source URL:** [NHLBI : Heart Failure Diagnosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart-failure/diagnosis).
- **License evidence:** **NHLBI-PD**, [exact policy](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/about/contact/trademark-branding-and-logo).
- **Attribution:** Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute; NIH; HHS.
- **Neutral accessible description:** A text-only inpatient case follows worsening breathing and fluid-retention clues; medication names, vitals, and test findings are labeled explicitly.
- **Review caveats:** Keep treatment choices conceptual unless a cardiology/pharmacy reviewer validates current dosing and renal adjustments. Exclude NHLBI page images and logos.

- **Attribution:** Source: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute; NIH; HHS.

- **Review caveats:** Pulmonary/EM review must set oxygen, ventilation, and admission thresholds. Do not reuse NHLBI graphics or branded multimedia.

### CA-IM-004 : Postoperative breathlessness and one swollen leg

- **Level/role:** Step 2; medicine/surgery clerk; bedside nurse; radiology workflow awareness.

- **Neutral accessible description:** A postoperative patient develops respiratory and leg symptoms; risk factors and findings are provided as readable text with no image dependence.
- **Review caveats:** Hematology/EM review is needed for probability tools, D-dimer use, imaging, anticoagulation, and pregnancy/renal branches. Do not reproduce CDC photographs.

### CA-IM-005 : Confusion is the first sepsis clue

- **Level/role:** Step 2; medicine/emergency clerk; RN, paramedic, and clinical-support staff.
- **Specialty:** Internal medicine, emergency medicine, infectious disease, patient safety, triage.
- **Artifact type:** Deterioration simulation with sequential vitals, urine-output trend, and escalation choices.
- **Original vignette seed:** An 81-year-old from home arrives for “weakness.” They are newly confused, tachypneic, hypotensive, and have urinary symptoms but no dramatic fever.
- **Learning objectives:** (1) Recognize sepsis despite an atypical temperature; (2) identify organ-dysfunction and perfusion clues; (3) initiate local sepsis escalation, cultures/source evaluation, and reassessment without waiting for every result.
- **Exact source URL:** [CDC : Caring for Patients with Sepsis](https://www.cdc.gov/sepsis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html).
- **License evidence:** **CDC-PD**, [Use of Agency Materials](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html).
- **Attribution:** Source: CDC. Source material is free at the linked page; no CDC/HHS endorsement is implied.
- **Neutral accessible description:** A time-sequenced text simulation makes subtle deterioration explicit through labeled changes in cognition, breathing, blood pressure, and urine output.
- **Review caveats:** Use the institution's current sepsis protocol; infectious-disease/critical-care review is required for cultures, fluids, antibiotics, vasopressors, and special populations. Avoid proprietary scores unless separately licensed.

### CA-IM-006 : New diabetes presenting as ketoacidosis

- **Level/role:** Step 2; medicine/pediatrics/emergency clerk; RN and laboratory staff awareness.
- **Specialty:** Endocrinology, internal medicine, pediatrics, emergency medicine.
- **Artifact type:** Progressive lab-interpretation vignette with labeled acid-base, glucose, ketone, and electrolyte data.

- **Review caveats:** Pediatric/endocrine review must validate all diagnostic cutoffs, fluid strategy, potassium safeguards, and cerebral-edema warnings. Text only; do not reuse NIDDK graphics.

### CA-IM-007 : Gastroenteritis, ibuprofen, and falling urine output

- **Level/role:** Step 2; medicine/family clerk; RN and pharmacist.
- **Specialty:** Nephrology, internal medicine, family medicine, medication safety.
- **Artifact type:** Medication-safety case with intake/output and creatinine trend.
- **Original vignette seed:** A 64-year-old with vomiting and diarrhea continued an ACE inhibitor and diuretic and added frequent over-the-counter ibuprofen. They now feel dizzy and are urinating less.

- **License evidence:** **NIDDK-PD**, [Copyright](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/copyright).
- **Attribution:** Source: NIDDK, NIH.
- **Neutral accessible description:** A medication timeline and urine/creatinine trend are presented in text and a screen-reader-friendly table.
- **Review caveats:** Nephrology/pharmacy review is needed for “sick day” messaging, medicine holds, fluid choices, and restart plans. Avoid giving universal stop-medication advice.

### CA-SU-013 : A signed form is not informed consent

- **Level/role:** Surgery/OB/procedure clerk; nurse, interpreter-services staff, and procedural team.
- **Specialty:** Surgery, patient safety, communication, ethics.
- **Artifact type:** Role-play case with choice points and a teach-back checkpoint.
- **Original vignette seed:** A patient scheduled for a procedure has signed the form but cannot explain the purpose, major risks, alternatives, or what declining would mean; a family member has been doing most of the talking.
- **Learning objectives:** (1) Separate a signature from an informed, voluntary choice; (2) explain options, benefits, harms, and alternatives neutrally; (3) elicit goals and confirm understanding with teach-back before reconfirming consent.
- **Exact source URL:** [AHRQ : Making Informed Consent an Informed Choice](https://www.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/professionals/systems/hospital/implementation-guide-making-informed-consent-informed-choice.pdf).
- **License evidence:** **AHRQ-IC-PD**, [item-level Public Domain Notice, PDF p. iii](https://www.ahrq.gov/sites/default/files/wysiwyg/professionals/systems/hospital/implementation-guide-making-informed-consent-informed-choice.pdf).
- **Attribution:** Shoemaker S, Brach C. *Implementation Guide for AHRQ's Making Informed Consent an Informed Choice*. AHRQ Publication No. 17-0061-1-EF, 2017.
- **Neutral accessible description:** A dialogue transcript identifies each speaker and provides all choices in plain text, with pauses learners can navigate by keyboard or screen reader.
- **Review caveats:** Local legal/ethics review is required for capacity, surrogacy, emergencies, and documentation. Use only the PDF's public-domain narrative; do not reproduce separately marked copyrighted materials or external module media.
